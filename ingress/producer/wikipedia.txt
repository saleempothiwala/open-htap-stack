Unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs), commonly known as drones, are aircraft without any human pilot, crew, or passengers on board.
The flight of UAVs may operate under remote control by a human operator, as remotely-piloted aircraft (RPA).
The first pilotless vehicles were built during or shortly after World War I.
Modern drones are used for missions ranging from military reconnaissance to commercial delivery.
Computer vision and artificial intelligence are often integrated into drone flight controllers.
Drones can be equipped with various sensors including high-resolution cameras, LIDAR, and thermal imaging.
The Oslo Airport at Gardermoen is a strictly restricted airspace for all unauthorized drone activity.
Drone racing has become a popular international sport with professional leagues and high-speed FPV viewing.
Advanced battery technology has significantly increased the flight duration of modern quadcopters.
Autonomous collision avoidance systems are becoming a standard feature in consumer drones.
Swarm robotics involving dozens of synchronized drones is a major research area in autonomous systems.
UAVs are frequently used in search and rescue missions to scout difficult terrain from the air.
The Royal Palace in Oslo is protected by a permanent no-fly zone to ensure public safety.
Agricultural drones help farmers monitor crop health through multispectral imaging and precision spraying.
Environmental scientists use drones to track wildlife migrations and monitor deforestation in remote areas.
The integration of 5G connectivity allows for ultra-low latency control of drones over long distances.
Drones are increasingly being used by utility companies to inspect power lines and wind turbines.
Regulations for drone flight vary significantly between different countries and urban environments.
Fornebu Tech Park is one of the designated testing grounds for next-generation urban air mobility.
The use of lightweight carbon fiber materials has improved the structural integrity and efficiency of UAVs.
